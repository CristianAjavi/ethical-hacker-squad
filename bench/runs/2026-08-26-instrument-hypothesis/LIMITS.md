# Limits of this round, written while the outcome is unknown

Committed before the scorer was run over the reports. Whatever the numbers turn out to be,
these were the round's weaknesses, and recording them afterwards would read as an excuse for
one outcome or a modesty flourish for the other.

## The blind is structural, not perfect

The tree was copied out with `.git` absent and the README replaced with a generic line. It is
still **identifiable**: `ckan/__init__.py` carries the version, and an arm reported reading it.
The pre-registration said version strings were left alone where removing them would change
code, and that is what happened — but the consequence is that "blinded" here means *no history
and no README*, not *unrecognisable*.

What stands between recognition and a contaminated result is the declared clause: an arm that
holds a published advisory for the project must say so and stop. One arm addressed the clause
explicitly and did not invoke it, on the stated ground that it could not name a specific
advisory for this project accurately. That is the clause behaving as designed, and it is also
the weakest link in the round: it depends on an arm's self-report.

**This limit is symmetric.** Both arms audit the same identifiable tree, so recognition cannot
favour one over the other. It bounds what the round can claim about absolute rates, not what it
can claim about the difference between the arms — which is the registered question.

## Arm A is not a straw man, and that cuts both ways

Arm A is the corpus as of `main`, which already carries `WEB-28`, its own mechanical
enumeration and the step-0 delivery fix. It is the product of three prior rounds of
improvement, not the starting point. A result where arm A does well is a result about a real
procedure.

## What a favourable result would still not license

- **No ranking.** No competitor arm ran. Nothing here compares this product to any other, and
  no sentence may be written from this data that does.
- **One class, one target, one round.** The registered band is about a single published
  advisory of a single class on a single repository.
- **The instrument is guaranteed to contain the answer.** The check flags both keyed sites
  deterministically, so arm B's ceiling is set by whether the agent *works the list*, not by
  whether the list is right. A high arm B measures the delivery of a worklist, not detection.

## What an unfavourable result would mean

If arm B misses, the failure is not in the check — it provably flags the site — but in the
agent working a 182-item list. That is a finding about this product and it goes in the ledger
next to the other four levers, not into a drawer.
