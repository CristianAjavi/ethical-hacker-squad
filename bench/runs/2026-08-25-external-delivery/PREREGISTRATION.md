# Pre-registration — the same advisory, with the step moved to where the agent starts

Committed **before the run**. Third round on this target, and the multiplicity that creates
is the first thing on the page rather than a footnote.

## The multiple-testing problem, stated before the number

This is the **third** measurement of the same corpus, on the same repository, against the
same key. The first scored 0 of 4. The second, after the enumeration step went into the
pack, scored 1 of 3 against a band of 2 of 4 and was published as refuted.

Running a fourth, and a fifth, until one lands inside a band is fishing, and it would make
every number on these pages worth nothing. So:

**This is the last round on this target for this intervention.** If it refutes, the position
published in `../2026-08-25-external-log-injection/` stands as the answer — on this class, on
real code, this corpus does not lead — and the next attempt has to change the target, not
retry the measurement.

## What the previous round established, and what it did not

`1 of 3` is a change from *never* to *once*, and it is below the band, so it was published as
a refutation. What it also showed is **where** the failure sits: two of the three runs read
`WEB-28`, had its enumeration step marked mandatory, and did not execute it.

That is not a defect in the procedure's text. It is a defect in delivery. The procedure is
prose in one of nineteen reference files, and `ehs-web-api.md` already carries a measured
warning that loading a pack costs budget the auditor then does not have for the target — an
arm that loaded its pack spent twice the budget to report a fifth as much.

## The change under test

`agents/ehs-web-api.md` — the agent's own entry point, read before any pack — now opens
**First actions** with a step `0`: produce the mechanical lists before opening anything.
Logging sinks, execution and interpolation sinks, and the request boundary. The step reports
its list sizes in the coverage declaration.

Every other first action is *read this file*. This is the only one that is *run this*, and
that is the whole hypothesis: an instruction placed where the auditor spends its opening
budget is executed, and the same instruction placed inside a pack it may never open is not.

## Design

Same target, `pyload/pyload` at `6c52b198d`, byte-identical, `.git` absent. Same key. Same
scoring rule. **Four runs.** The auditor is pointed at `agents/ehs-web-api.md` as its
instructions rather than told to read references generically, because the change under test
lives there and a round that did not read it would be measuring nothing.

## Prediction, with a band

**At least 3 of 4 runs report the advisory.** The band is deliberately higher than the one
that failed. If moving a step from a file the auditor might read to the file it always reads
produces the same 1-in-3, the placement is not the variable and this line of work is done.

**Claims stay above 15 across the four runs.** The previous two rounds produced 25 and 24. A
step that collapses the rest of the audit to buy this one defect is a trade, not a fix.

## What refutes it

- **Fewer than 3 of 4.** Placement was not the variable. The intervention is recorded as
  unmeasured-or-ineffective, this target is retired for it, and the honest position stays the
  one already published.
- **Claims collapse below 15.** Reported as a trade with both numbers printed, and the step
  is reverted rather than kept.
- **Fewer than 3 parseable reports.** The round could not measure, and — because of the
  multiplicity above — it is **not** re-run to reach the count.

## What each outcome forces

- **Prediction holds.** The claim is exactly: *placing a mechanical enumeration step at the
  agent's entry point recovered a published advisory in 3 of 4 runs where the same corpus
  scored 0 of 4 without it, in one repository.* One repository, one advisory. It does not
  become a claim about detection, and `0 of 4` stays on its page as the measurement it was.
- **Prediction refutes.** Three rounds will have failed to move this, and the ledger records
  that the class this project named is one it does not find in real code — which is what the
  external round already says, and what the README will keep saying.
